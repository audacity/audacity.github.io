An audio track's control panel carries, from the top: the track type icon,
the editable track name, and the track menu button. Below the name sits a row
holding the pan knob, the volume slider and the mute and solo buttons, and
below that a full-width **Effects** button. A pair of level meters runs down
the right-hand edge of the panel — one bar for a mono track, two for a stereo
track.

Reduce the track's height far enough and the panel collapses. The pan knob,
volume slider and Effects button are hidden, and the mute and solo buttons
move up to sit beside the track name so they stay reachable.

Everything on the panel except the meters can also be reached with the
keyboard: tab moves from the track itself into its header controls, and then
on to the clips in the track.

## Mute

**Mute** is a toggle button on an audio track's control panel, next to
[Solo](#solo). While it is switched on the track is not heard during playback.
Nothing about the audio itself changes — muting is a playback state, and it
can be switched on and off at any time.

A muted track's level meters read nothing, since no signal is leaving the
track.

Mute is stored per track and saved with the project.

When the track is collapsed, mute and solo move up beside the track name so
they remain available.

## Solo

**Solo** is a toggle button on an audio track's control panel, next to
[Mute](#mute). While any track in the project is soloed, only the soloed
tracks are heard on playback; everything else is held back. Soloing more than
one track lets you listen to that group together.

Solo is a playback state and changes nothing about the audio, so it can be
switched on and off freely while you work.

Solo is stored per track and saved with the project.

When the track is collapsed, solo and mute move up beside the track name so
they remain available.

## Volume

The volume slider sets how loud an audio track plays back, relative to the
audio it contains. It runs from **-60 dB** to **+12 dB**, with 0 dB — no
change — in the middle of that range. A tooltip follows the handle while you
drag, showing the current value in dB.

- Drag the handle, or click anywhere along the slider, to set a value.
- Hold **Shift** while dragging for fine adjustment; the slider then moves far
  more slowly than the pointer.
- Double-click the slider to reset the track to 0 dB.
- With the slider focused, the up and down keys step the value.

The slider is hidden while the track is collapsed. It affects playback only —
the audio in the track is left untouched.

## Panning

The pan knob places an audio track in the stereo field. It runs from **100
left** through centre to **100 right**, in steps of 1. A tooltip appears while
you use it, reading `Pan:` followed by the value — for example `50L` or
`50R`. At the centre position the value is shown with no direction letter.

- Drag the knob to set a value. Dragging works both vertically and
  horizontally.
- Double-click the knob to return the track to centre.
- With the knob focused, the up and down keys step the value by 1.

The knob is hidden while the track is collapsed. Panning is a playback
setting; the audio in the track is not altered.

## Meters

A narrow vertical meter runs down the right-hand edge of every audio track's
control panel, showing the level leaving that track while it plays or records.
A mono track has one bar; a stereo track has two side by side, the left
channel first.

Each bar shows the current peak level with an RMS reading drawn over it, plus
a marker holding the highest peak reached. If the signal clips, the bar
switches to its clipped colour and stays there until the meter is cleared.

Click the meters to clear them, which resets both the peak-hold marker and the
clipping indication. They are also cleared automatically when playback or
recording starts, and when the track loses focus.

The meters read in dBFS, loudest at the top. A muted track shows no level.

## Effects

**Effects** is a full-width button across the bottom of an audio track's
control panel. It opens the effects panel for that track, where its chain of
realtime effects is built and managed.

The panel shows the track's name with a power button beside it that bypasses
or re-enables the whole chain at once, the list of effects currently on the
track, and an **Add effect** button that opens the list of available effects.
Individual effects in the list can be bypassed, reordered and removed.

Realtime effects are non-destructive: they are applied as the track plays, so
they can be changed or removed at any time without altering the audio.

The button is hidden while the track is collapsed, or when the track is too
short for it to fit.

See [Apply your first effect](/manual/getting-started/apply-your-first-effect) for
a fuller walkthrough.

## Track options

Every audio track has a menu holding the options that do not fit on the
control panel itself. Open it with the menu button beside the track name, by
right-clicking anywhere on the control panel, or — with the track focused —
with <Shortcut client:load keys="shift+f10" />.

On a mono track the three stereo channel commands are replaced by **Make
stereo track**, which is available only when exactly one track is selected and
the track directly below it is also mono. It combines the two into a single
stereo track.

### Rename, Duplicate and Delete

The track menu opens with three commands that act on the whole track.

- **Rename** puts the track's name into edit mode in the control panel. Type
  the new name and confirm it.
- **Duplicate** makes a copy of the track, with its audio, and adds it to the
  project.
- **Delete** removes the track and everything in it from the project.

Renaming can also be started without the menu: double-click the track name in
the control panel.

All three are undoable.

### Move track

**Move track** is a submenu of the track menu holding four commands:

- **Move track up** — swap the track with the one above it.
- **Move track down** — swap it with the one below.
- **Move track to top** — send it to the top of the project.
- **Move track to bottom** — send it to the bottom.

None of the four has a default keyboard shortcut; one can be assigned on the
Shortcuts page in Preferences.

Tracks can also be reordered by dragging a control panel up or down. A drop
indicator shows where the track will land.

### Track visualization

In the current Audacity 4 alpha this submenu is called **Track
visualization**. It sets how the selected track draws its audio, and holds:

- **Waveform** — the familiar amplitude view.
- **Spectrogram** — frequency content over time.
- **Multi-view** — waveform and spectrogram together, stacked in the one
  track.
- **Half-wave** — draw only the upper half of the waveform.
- **Spectrogram settings…** — opens the spectrogram settings for this track.

Waveform, Spectrogram and Multi-view are mutually exclusive; the one in use is
ticked. Half-wave is a separate toggle that can be on alongside any of them.

The choice applies to this track only.

### Track color

**Track color** is a submenu of the track menu offering nine colours: Blue,
Violet, Magenta, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Turquoise and Cyan. Choosing one
sets the colour the track's clips are drawn in.

The colour currently in use is ticked, so the submenu doubles as a way of
seeing which colour a track has been given.

Colour is a display property — it changes nothing about the audio. It is
stored per track and saved with the project, and the change is undoable.

### Show vertical rulers

**Show vertical rulers** is a tick-box item in the track menu. When it is on,
a column of vertical rulers appears to the right of the tracks, giving each
track a scale for the audio drawn in it. Turning it off hides the column and
gives the space back to the waveforms.

Although it is reached from a single track's menu, this is an application-wide
setting rather than a per-track one: switching it on or off from any track
affects the whole project view, and the setting persists between sessions.

### Meters & monitoring

**Meters & monitoring** is a submenu of the track menu with two tick-box
items:

- **Show mic metering** — display the level coming in from the recording
  input, so you can set levels before you record.
- **Turn on input monitoring** — pass the input through to your output so you
  can hear it live.

Both are application-wide settings rather than per-track ones. They are
reached from a track's menu, but switching either on or off applies to the
application as a whole and persists between sessions.

Input monitoring can cause feedback if you are monitoring on speakers rather
than headphones.

### Swap stereo channels

**Swap stereo channels** exchanges the two channels of a stereo track: what
was on the left is heard on the right, and the other way round. It is useful
when a recording has come in with its channels the wrong way round. The
command does not appear on mono or label tracks.

It acts on the selected tracks, so several stereo tracks can be swapped at
once. The change is undoable.

### Split stereo to L/R mono

**Split stereo to L/R mono** turns a stereo track into two separate mono
tracks, one carrying the left channel and one the right. The two are panned
hard left and hard right, so playback still sounds as it did before the split
— but each channel can now be edited, and given its own volume, effects and
mute or solo state, on its own. The command does not appear on mono or label
tracks.

If you want the two channels panned centre instead, use
[Split stereo to center mono](#split-stereo-to-center-mono).

### Split stereo to center mono

**Split stereo to center mono** turns a stereo track into two separate mono
tracks, one carrying the left channel and one the right, with both panned to
the centre. Each channel then plays through both speakers and can be edited,
levelled and processed independently. It does not appear on mono or label
tracks.

Because both tracks come out centred, the stereo image of the original
recording is lost. To keep it, use
[Split stereo to L/R mono](#split-stereo-to-lr-mono) instead.

### Format, Rate and Resampling

The last group of the track menu holds three items that deal with how a
track's audio is stored.

**Format:** is a submenu offering **16-bit PCM**, **24-bit PCM** and **32-bit
Float**. The one in use is ticked, and the submenu's own title shows it — for
example _Format: 32-bit Float_.

**Rate:** is a submenu listing the sample rates your current audio device
supports, plus **Other…** for a rate that is not on the list. As with Format,
the title shows the track's current rate — for example _Rate: 44100 Hz_ — and
the rate in use is ticked. If the track is at a rate the device does not
offer, **Other…** is the item shown as ticked.

**Resample track…** converts the audio in the track to a different sample
rate.

Format and Rate are both disabled while a recording is in progress.
