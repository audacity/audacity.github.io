The terms this manual uses, in alphabetical order.

## Clip

A contiguous piece of audio on a track. Clips can be moved, trimmed, split,
joined, grouped and renamed independently of the track that holds them, and
a track can hold any number of them.

## Destructive effect

An effect applied from the Effect menu that rewrites the selected audio.
Undo restores the previous state, but the effect is otherwise baked into
the clip — unlike a realtime effect.

## Envelope

A line drawn over a clip that changes its volume over time. Adding and
moving envelope points automates fades and level changes without altering
the underlying audio.

## Hotbar

The row of quick-access controls above the track area, holding tools such
as Share audio alongside the transport controls.

## Label track

A track type that holds text markers instead of audio. Labels mark points
or ranges in the project and can be exported as a text file.

## Loop region

A range on the timeline that playback repeats while looping is enabled.
The loop region is set from a selection or dragged out on the timeline
ruler.

## Playhead

The vertical line across the tracks showing where playback is, or where it
will start. Clicking in the timeline ruler moves it.

## Project

Everything you have open in one Audacity window: the tracks, clips,
labels, envelopes and settings, saved together as a single project file.

## Realtime effect

An effect that processes audio live during playback without changing the
audio itself. Realtime effects stack on a track and can be reordered,
bypassed or removed at any time.

## Sample rate

How many audio samples per second a track or project uses, in hertz.
Higher rates capture higher frequencies but produce larger files.

## Spectrogram

A view of a track that shows frequency content over time as colour
intensity, instead of the waveform's amplitude shape. Useful for spotting
noise and editing specific frequencies.

## Timeline

The ruler above the tracks that maps horizontal position to time. It hosts
the playhead, the loop region and click-to-seek.

## Track

A horizontal lane in the project that holds audio clips (mono or stereo)
or labels. Each audio track has its own controls for volume, pan, mute and
solo in the track control panel.

## Track control panel

The panel at the left edge of each track with its name, volume and pan
controls, mute and solo buttons, and the track's options menu.

## Workspace

A saved arrangement of Audacity's panels and toolbars. Switching workspace
changes the layout to suit a task — editing, recording or mixing — without
touching the project.
