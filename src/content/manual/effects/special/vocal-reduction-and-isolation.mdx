Vocal Reduction and Isolation was an Audacity 3 Nyquist plug-in that tried to
remove or extract a centred vocal from a stereo mix by cancelling what the two
channels had in common. **Audacity 4 does not ship it**, and there is no Vocal
Reduction and Isolation entry in the Effect menu.

## What to use instead

The underlying technique is still available by hand. Split the stereo track
into two mono tracks, apply
[Invert](/manual/effects/special/invert) to one of them, and
mix the two together: everything identical in both channels cancels, which
usually takes out most of a centred lead vocal. What is left is mono, and
anything else sitting in the centre — often the bass and the kick — goes with
the vocal.

For removing a specific sound rather than everything in the centre, the
[Spectral Tools](/manual/effects/spectral-tools) act on a
chosen band of frequencies over a chosen stretch of time, which is a more
surgical approach.

## Notes

None of these methods separates a vocal cleanly. They work on how the mix was
assembled rather than on what the instruments are, so results depend entirely
on the recording — and any reverb on the vocal, which is rarely centred, stays
behind.
