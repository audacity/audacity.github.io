Splits the audio into a bank of frequency bands, tracks how the level in each
band changes over time, and uses those envelopes to shape a carrier signal.
The familiar result is the robot voice: speech articulation carried by a
synthetic tone.

## Where to find it

**Effect → Distortion and modulation → Vocoder**.

## Setting up the track

What is used as the carrier depends on the track. On a **stereo** track, the
left channel is the modulator and the **right channel is used as the
carrier** — put the speech left and the synth or pad right. On a **mono**
track there is no separate carrier, so the effect builds one from sine waves,
white noise and radar needles according to the amplitude controls below.

## Controls

- **Distance: (1 to 120, default = 20)** — 1 to 120, default 20. Sets how
  quickly each band's level envelope responds. Low values track the modulator
  tightly and keep consonants crisp; high values smooth the envelopes out, so
  the effect sounds more distant and less articulate.
- **Output choice** — Both Channels or Right Only. Both Channels is the
  default.
- **Number of vocoder bands** — 10 to 240, default 40. More bands follow the
  modulator's spectrum more faithfully; fewer bands sound coarser and more
  obviously synthetic.
- **Amplitude of carrier wave (percent)** — 0 to 100, default 100.
- **Amplitude of white noise (percent)** — 0 to 100, default 0.
- **Amplitude of Radar Needles (percent)** — 0 to 100, default 0.
- **Frequency of Radar Needles (Hz)** — 1 to 100, default 30.

## Notes

The three amplitude controls set the mix of the generated carrier on a mono
track. Adding white noise puts breath and sibilance back into a vocoded
voice, which otherwise loses its consonants; radar needles add a periodic
pulse whose rate is set by the frequency control, giving a buzzier, more
pitched carrier.

For intelligible speech, keep the band count reasonably high and add a little
noise. For a deliberately crude, early-vocoder sound, drop the band count.
