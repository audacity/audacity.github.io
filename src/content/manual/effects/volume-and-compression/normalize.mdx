Normalize applies one gain change so that the loudest peak in the selection lands on a level you choose. It can also re-centre audio that is sitting off the zero line. Use it to bring a recording up to a consistent peak level before further editing.

<UIShot name="effects/Normalize" alt="The Normalize dialog" />

## Where to find it

**Effect → Volume and compression → Normalize**, with audio selected.

## Controls

- **Remove DC offset (center on 0.0 vertically)** — shift the waveform so its average sits on zero. On by default.
- **Normalize peak amplitude to** — set the highest peak in the selection to this level, in dB. The value cannot be above 0 dB; the default is −1 dB.
- **Normalize stereo channels independently** — normalise the left and right channels separately rather than applying one gain to the pair. Off by default, and available only when peak normalisation is switched on.

## Notes

At least one of the two checkboxes has to be ticked before the effect can be applied — with both off there is nothing for it to do.

DC offset shows up as a waveform whose centre line is visibly above or below zero. It wastes headroom and can cause clicks at clip boundaries, which is why removing it is on by default.

A target of −1 dB rather than 0 dB leaves a little headroom, which is safer for material that will later be encoded to a lossy format.

Normalising the channels independently changes the stereo balance, so leave it off for ordinary stereo material.

If you only want the DC correction and nothing else, the separate **Remove DC offset** effect does that on its own.
