A high [dynamic range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_range_compression) can lead to quiet parts of a track being too quiet compared to its loud parts, causing the listener to constantly adjust the volume to keep things at an acceptable level. Audacity has several tools to help reduce the dynamic range and increase the loudness of a track.

## Compressor

<UIShot
  name="effects/Compressor-realtime"
  alt="The Compressor as a realtime effect in a track's effects stack, with its graph and controls"
/>

Add it as a realtime effect from the track's **Effects** button → **Add effect** → **Compressor**, or apply it to the selected audio from **Effect → Volume and compression → Compressor**. The [Compressor](/manual/effects/volume-and-compression/compressor) page describes each control.

The compressor can be used to reduce the dynamic range by a certain ratio for all sounds that exceed the threshold. Doing so will make the resulting audio quieter, so you need to add make-up gain to make up for that, or, alternatively, normalize your track in a second step. The knee width and smoothing parameters are there to reduce distortion.

<BestPractices>

- To get started, try a **factory preset** from the preset dropdown. This effect comes with useful presets for a variety of content.
- Use this effect as a [master effect or realtime effect](/manual/getting-started/apply-your-first-effect) to see a graph of it working as the audio plays: the line dips each time the compressor engages.
- Lower the threshold if the compressor isn't doing anything. However, you typically don't want the compressor to _always_ be engaged.

</BestPractices>

## Limiter

<UIShot
  name="effects/Limiter-realtime"
  alt="The Limiter as a realtime effect in a track's effects stack"
/>

A limiter is effectively identical to a compressor, with the difference that it's much harsher: while a compressor uses a low ratio and may let a sound exceed the line drawn on the curve temporarily, a limiter does not allow any sound to exceed the threshold. The [Limiter](/manual/effects/volume-and-compression/limiter) page describes its controls.

<BestPractices>

- To get started, try a **factory preset** from the **Presets & settings** button. This effect comes with useful presets for a variety of content.
- Use this effect as a [master effect](/manual/getting-started/apply-your-first-effect) to see a graph of it working as the audio plays, and to prevent your project from clipping.
- Typically, limiters are used as a "last line of defense" to prevent clipping.

</BestPractices>

## Plugins for this

Audacity's Compressor and Limiter cover most material. For a different
character, a multiband compressor or a loudness-matching limiter,
<a href={museHubURL("/category/plugin", "manual-dynamic-range")}>MuseHub</a>
has third-party dynamics plugins that run as realtime effects in the same
way.
