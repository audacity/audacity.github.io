[Basic audio editing](/manual/getting-started/basic-audio-editing) worked on
stretches of audio inside a clip. This step moves up a level: the clip itself
as an object you pick up, split, and arrange. A finished edit is usually just
a handful of clips in the right order with the silences closed up — and none
of it touches the audio inside them.

## Select clips, not stretches

Click a clip's **header** — the strip along its top — to select the whole
clip. Hold **Shift** and click more headers to gather several, and they don't
need to be neighbours: a clip at the start and one at the end can be selected
together, and whatever you do next applies to all of them.

<UIShot
  name="trackpanel/WaveClip-selected"
  alt="A selected clip, showing the trim handles and speed handles at its edges"
/>

The handles that appear on selection work on every selected clip at once —
trim three clips to the beat in one drag.

## Move them into place

Drag a clip **by its header** to move it:

- **Along its track** to change when it plays.
- **Onto another track** to change what it plays alongside.
- **Into the empty space below the tracks** to give it a brand-new track of
  its own.

Switch on **Snap** in the toolbar first if you want clips to land exactly
against each other or on whole seconds — loose ends between clips are the
usual source of mystery silences.

<Callout type="warning" title="Clips can swallow each other">
  Dropping a clip on top of another clip is allowed: the one underneath is eaten
  into where they overlap. Sometimes that is exactly what you want — a retake
  pasted over a flub — but if it wasn't,
  <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+z" /> puts it back.
</Callout>

## Split, then rearrange

Splitting is how one long take becomes clips you can arrange: press

<Shortcut client:load keys="s" /> and click where the cut should go, or put the
cursor there and press <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+i" /> — the same moves as
[basic audio editing](/manual/getting-started/basic-audio-editing), used now to
make *pieces* rather than to delete. Split before and after the part you want to
keep, and each piece has its own header: drag the keeper into place, delete the
rest, and close the gaps with a ripple delete.

## Name them while you still remember

Double-click a clip's header to rename it. "Intro take 3" beats "Audio 1"
the moment a project has more than two clips in it — future-you, returning
to the project next week, is the beneficiary.

## Everything else lives on the right-click

A clip's context menu holds the rest of its powers: cut and copy, duplicate,
the split and delete variants, pitch and speed — and **Clip color**, which is
worth adopting early: colour the keeper takes green and the doubtful ones
red, and the timeline becomes its own to-do list.

/>

<Callout type="tip" title="A second track is free scratch space">
  Not sure yet where a clip belongs? Drag it to a spare track below and park it
  there. Tracks cost nothing, everything on them stays in sync as you edit, and
  muting the scratch track keeps it out of your ears until you decide.
</Callout>
