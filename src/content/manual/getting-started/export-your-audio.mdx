## Export a file

Open **File → Export audio**, or press
<Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+shift+e" />.

<UIShot
  name="app/SceneExport"
  alt="The Export audio dialog: export type, file name, folder and format, audio options, and the Export button"
/>

The **Type** dropdown at the top decides what gets exported:

- **Export full project audio** mixes everything in the project into the
  file. This is the usual choice for a finished edit.
- **Export selected audio** exports only the current selection. Useful for
  pulling one answer out of an interview, or checking how a stretch sounds
  in the finished format.
- **Export audio in loop region** exports the stretch marked by the loop
  region on the timeline.

Below that, name the file, choose where it goes, and pick a format. The
dialog also lets you choose mono or stereo, the sample rate, and, for
formats like MP3, the bit rate.

<Callout type="info">
  Some export formats, including M4A, need [FFmpeg
  installed](/manual/basics/installing-ffmpeg) before they appear.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="A prompt about folder access">
  The first time you export into a folder like Documents, your computer may ask
  whether Audacity is allowed to access it. Allow it, or the export cannot be
  written there.
</Callout>

## Which settings?

Your destination may have its own guidance on format, file size or
loudness, and if it does, follow that. If nobody has told you otherwise, MP3
is the format everything accepts: 128 kbps is a safe default for speech, and
256 kbps for music.

Beyond that default, three tips go a long way:

- **A single voice wants mono.** Stereo doubles the file for two identical
  channels — save it for music and anything recorded in stereo.
- **Opus and M4A beat MP3** at the same file size, if your destination
  accepts them.
- **FLAC and WavPack shrink WAV by up to half without losing anything** —
  the archival choice when quality matters and MP3 will not do.
