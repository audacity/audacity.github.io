button.

## Your first take

1. Press **Record**, or <Shortcut client:load keys="r" />. A **track**
   appears and a clip begins to draw as you speak. A track is a horizontal
   lane holding audio — you did not have to make it: Audacity created one
   because you needed somewhere to record.
2. Record a few seconds of silence at the start of every session. It gives
   you room to trim, and a sample of your room's own sound for noise
   reduction later.
3. Press **Stop** when you are done, then <Shortcut client:load keys="space" />
   to play it back from the playhead.
4. If you fluff a line, pause, leave a beat of silence, and say it again.
   Cutting the bad take out later is far quicker than starting over.

## Check the take

Look at the waveform as well as listening to it — the shape tells you things
your ears will not, especially on laptop speakers.

- **A solid block filling the height of the track** means it was recorded too
  loud, and the peaks are clipped. Clipping is distortion, and it cannot be
  repaired afterwards: lower the record level and take it again.

<UIShot
  name="trackpanel/WaveClipTakeClipped"
  alt="A clipped take: the waveform is a solid block with flat tops where the peaks were cut off"
/>

- **A nearly flat line** means it was too quiet. Raise the level or move
  closer and take it again. A take you cannot repeat can be lifted with the
  **Amplify** effect, though that raises the room noise along with the voice.

<UIShot
  name="trackpanel/WaveClipTakeQuiet"
  alt="A take recorded too quietly: the waveform is barely visible around the centre line"
/>

- **Clear movement with the peaks well short of the top** is what you are
  after.

<UIShot
  name="trackpanel/WaveClipTakeHealthy"
  alt="A healthy take: clear waveform movement with the peaks well short of the top of the track"
/>

## Before you press record

- **Get closer.** A hand-span from the microphone gives you far more voice
  and far less room than sitting back from it.
- **Speak across it, not into it.** Angling the microphone slightly off
  your mouth stops P and B sounds thumping.
- **Silence the room.** Fans, fridges, air conditioning and anything humming
  on the desk all end up in the recording.
- **Soften the space.** Curtains, a bed, a rug, clothes on rails — soft
  things absorb the reflections that make a recording sound like a bathroom.
  It is much easier to avoid reflections than to remove them later.

<Callout type="tip" title="Test before you commit">
  Record ten seconds and play it back before recording anything you care
  about. Every fix is easier now than after a full session — especially a
  wrong input device or a clipped level, neither of which can be repaired
  afterwards.
</Callout>

## Recording onto another track

Once a take is down, you may want to record more alongside it — a guest, a
second attempt, or music under an introduction. **Add track**, at the top of
the track area, makes an empty track ready for the next voice, and offers a
**mono** track, a **stereo** track, or a **label** track (text markers
rather than audio).

<UIShot
  name="flyouts/AddNewTrackPopup"
  state="open"
  alt="The Add track choices: a mono track, a stereo track, or a label track"
/>

Once you have more than one track, it matters which is **selected**: click a
track's header and it highlights, and that is the track your next recording
goes onto. Get into the habit of clicking the track you mean before you
press Record — if a take turns up somewhere unexpected, that is almost
always why.

Recording starts on the selected track with **Record**, or on a brand-new
track with **Record → Record on new track**.
