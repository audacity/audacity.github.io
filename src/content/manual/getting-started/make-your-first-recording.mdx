## Create a track

When you record, the audio is stored in a **clip**. Clips live on
**tracks**, the horizontal lanes that run along the timeline. **Add track**,
at the top of the track area, is where you add one of the track types.

<UIShot
  name="app/SceneAddTrack"
  alt="The Add track button on an empty project, with its choices open beneath it: Mono, Stereo, and Label"
/>

- **Mono** holds one channel of audio. One microphone produces one channel,
  so a voice wants a mono track.
- **Stereo** holds two channels. Use it for music and for sources that are
  already stereo.
- **Label** holds text markers rather than audio.

Pick **Mono** and the track appears: its name and controls in the header on
the left, and an empty lane stretching along the timeline. This is where
your recording will appear.

<UIShot
  name="app/SceneMonoTrack"
  alt="A project with a single mono track: the track header on the left and an empty lane along the timeline"
/>

## Before you record

- **Select the track.** Recording goes onto the selected track: click a
  track's header and it highlights. If you skip this step entirely, pressing
  Record creates a track for you.
- **Check your level.** Your normal speech should peak between **−18 dB and
  −6 dB** on the meter. Setting the level, and hearing yourself while you
  record, are covered in
  [Connect your microphone](/manual/getting-started/connect-your-microphone).
- **Get closer.** A hand-span from the microphone gives you far more voice
  and far less room than sitting back from it.
- **Speak across it, not into it.** Angling the microphone slightly off
  your mouth stops P and B sounds thumping.
- **Silence the room.** Fans, fridges, air conditioning and anything humming
  on the desk all end up in the recording.
- **Soften the space.** Curtains, a bed, a rug, and clothes on rails absorb
  the reflections that make a recording sound like a bathroom. Avoiding
  reflections is much easier than removing them later.

## Recording

The round red **Record** button sits in the transport controls, at the left
of the top bar.

<UIShot
  name="app/SceneSpotlightRecord"
  alt="The transport controls with the round red Record button ringed"
/>

- **Record**, or <Shortcut client:load keys="r" />, starts recording on the
  selected track, and a clip begins to draw as you speak.
- **Pause** holds the recording without ending it. Press it again to
  continue in the same clip.
- **Stop**, or <Shortcut client:load keys="space" />, ends the take.
  <Shortcut client:load keys="space" /> then plays it back from the
  playhead.

## Your first take

1. Record a few seconds of silence at the start of every session. It gives
   you room to trim, and a sample of your room's own sound for noise
   reduction later.
2. Say your piece, then stop and play it back.
3. If you make a mistake, pause your speech, leave a moment of silence, and
   say the line again. Cutting the mistake out later is quicker than
   starting over.

## Check the take

Look at the waveform as well as listening to it. The shape shows problems
that are hard to hear, especially on laptop speakers.

- **A solid block filling the height of the track** means it was recorded too
  loud, and the peaks are clipped. Clipping is distortion, and it cannot be
  repaired afterwards: lower the record level and take it again.
  **View → Show clipping in waveform** marks the clipped samples in red on
  the waveform.

<UIShot
  name="trackpanel/WaveClipTakeClipped"
  alt="A clipped take: the waveform is a solid block with flat tops where the peaks were cut off"
/>

- **A nearly flat line** means it was too quiet. Raise the level or move
  closer and take it again. A take you cannot repeat can be lifted with the
  **Amplify** effect, though that raises the room noise along with the voice.

<UIShot
  name="trackpanel/WaveClipTakeQuiet"
  alt="A take recorded too quietly: the waveform is barely visible around the centre line"
/>

- **Clear movement with the peaks well short of the top** is a healthy
  take.

<UIShot
  name="trackpanel/WaveClipTakeHealthy"
  alt="A healthy take: clear waveform movement with the peaks well short of the top of the track"
/>

<Callout type="tip" title="Test before you commit">
  Record ten seconds and play it back before recording anything you care
  about. Every fix is easier now than after a full session. A wrong input
  device or a clipped level cannot be repaired afterwards.
</Callout>
