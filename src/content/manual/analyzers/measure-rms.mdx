Measure RMS calculates the root mean square level of the selected audio and
reports it in decibels. RMS tracks how loud something actually sounds far
better than the peak level does, so it is the number to compare when you are
matching one recording against another.

## Where to find it

**Analyze → Measure RMS**, with audio selected.

There are no settings: the effect measures the selection and shows the result.

## What it reports

For a mono selection, one figure, labelled **Mono**.

For a stereo selection, three: **Left** and **Right** for the two channels
separately, and **Stereo** for the two combined.

## Notes

Nothing is changed and nothing is added to the project — the measurement
appears as a message and that is all.

The figure covers the whole selection, so a selection that includes silence or
long pauses reads lower than the passage you were interested in. Select a
representative stretch of the actual material.

Two recordings matched by RMS will sound about equally loud even if their peak
levels differ considerably, which is why this measurement, rather than the peak,
is the one to use before mixing takes together.
