# Tools toolbar

The [three toolbars](/manual/manual-index/toolbars) split the window between
them; this one is where the work happens — playback and recording, the
editing tools, zoom, the playhead position, snapping and the record level.
It is also the only toolbar you can move.

  name="app/ToolbarTools"
  alt="The Tools toolbar: transport controls, editing tools, zoom, the playhead timecode, snapping and the record level button"
/>

## Moving the Tools toolbar

Grab the Transport Toolbar's drag handle and drag it:

- Drop it near the top of the window to dock it at the top.
- Drop it near the bottom of the window to dock it at the bottom.
- Drop it anywhere else to leave it floating at that position.

The Project Toolbar and Selection Toolbar stay fixed in place.

## Navigating toolbars with the keyboard

Once a toolbar has focus, arrow keys move between its buttons and controls. See Accessibility and Keyboard Navigation for the full keyboard navigation model, including an alternative mode for screen reader users.

## Play

Starts playback from the edit cursor, or from the start of the selection if
you have one.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="play" />

Once playback is running the same button becomes Pause — press it to stop at
the current position, and again to carry on from there.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="playing" />

- **No selection** — plays from the edit cursor to the end of the project.
- **A selection** — plays the selected region only.
- **A loop region enabled** — plays the loop region and repeats it. See
  [Toggle Loop Region](#toggle-loop-region).

Playback follows whichever tracks are unmuted. A soloed track plays on its
own; see the Track Control Panel for mute and solo.

## Stop

Stops playback or recording. The playhead returns to where playback started
rather than staying where you stopped it.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="stop" />

Stopping ends a recording and keeps what was captured — the audio stays in
the track. Stopping is not undoing: to discard a take, stop first and then
undo.

## Record

Starts recording from the currently selected input. While recording, the
button shows its active state.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="record" />

While a take is running the button turns red, so you can tell recording is
live without watching the meter. Pressing it again stops the recording.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="recording" />

Before you record, check the input device and the record level — a signal
that never moves the meter won't be captured, and one that pins it will clip.
See [Record Level](#record-level) for the meter, and Audio setup in the
Project Toolbar for choosing the input.

> **Tip:** If you're recording for the first time,
> [Connect your microphone](/manual/getting-started/connect-your-microphone)
> walks through the whole setup rather than just this button.

Recording adds audio to a track. Where it lands depends on your selection and
which track is focused — see the Track Control Panel pages for adding and
selecting tracks.

## Rewind to Start

Moves the playhead backwards. In the Transport Toolbar the button is
labelled **Step backward** and carries a skip-back icon.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="step-backward" />

The button is unavailable while playback is running — stop first, then step.
It is present in every workspace.

## Rewind to End

Moves the playhead forwards. In the Transport Toolbar the button is labelled
**Step forward** and carries a skip-forward icon.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="step-forward" />

Like Step backward, it is unavailable while playback is running. It is
present in every workspace.

## Toggle Loop Region

Turns the loop region on and off. Unlike Play, Stop and Record, this is a
toggle: it stays lit while looping is on, and the button reflects that state
rather than firing once and returning to rest.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="loop" />

While looping is on it stays lit:

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="loop-on" />

With looping on, playback repeats the loop region rather than continuing to
the end of the project. The region itself is set on the timeline — see the
Timeline pages for creating and clearing a loop region, and for whether
creating a loop also changes your selection.

Turning looping off leaves the region defined but inactive, so you can switch
it back on without setting it up again.

## Clip Gain

Turns on envelope editing, so you can draw volume changes across a clip
rather than setting one level for the whole thing. The button is labelled
**Clip envelope** in the toolbar.

<UIExample component="toggle-tool-button" variant="envelope" />

It is a toggle: it stays pressed while envelope editing is on, and clicking
it again returns you to normal editing.

<UIExample component="toggle-tool-button" variant="envelope-on" />

It appears in the Classic, Modern and Music workspaces; the Spectral editing
workspace leaves it out.

> **Tip:** A clip's envelope is drawn on the clip itself, so you can see the
> shape you have made without leaving the track.

## Split Tool

Turns on the split tool. While it is on, clicking a clip splits it at that
point instead of selecting it. The button is labelled **Cut / Split** in the
toolbar.

<UIExample component="toggle-tool-button" variant="split" />

It is a toggle and stays pressed while the tool is active:

<UIExample component="toggle-tool-button" variant="split-on" />

The current design gives it the **S** key.

It appears in the Classic, Modern and Music workspaces.

## Toggle Spectral View

Switches the track display from a waveform to a spectrogram, which shows
frequency against time instead of amplitude against time. The button is
labelled **Spectral view** in the toolbar.

<UIExample component="toggle-tool-button" variant="spectral" />

It is a toggle and stays pressed while spectral view is on:

<UIExample component="toggle-tool-button" variant="spectral-on" />

The Spectral editing workspace is already in spectral view, so instead of a
Spectral view button it offers the reverse — a **Waveform** button that takes
you back:

<UIExample component="toggle-tool-button" variant="waveform" />

It appears in the Classic and Modern workspaces; the Music workspace leaves
it out, and Spectral editing replaces it with the Waveform toggle described
above.

## Zoom In

Zooms the timeline in, showing a shorter stretch of the project in more
detail.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="zoom-in" />

Zoom in, Zoom out and Zoom toggle are the three zoom controls present in
every workspace.

## Zoom Out

Zooms the timeline out, fitting a longer stretch of the project on screen.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="zoom-out" />

Like Zoom in and Zoom toggle, it is present in every workspace.

## Zoom to Selection

Zooms so the current selection fills the visible timeline. The button is
labelled **Fit selection** in the toolbar.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="fit-selection" />

It needs a selection to work on — see the Selection Toolbar at the bottom of
the window for the current selection's start, end and duration.

It appears in the Classic workspace only; the Modern, Music and Spectral
editing workspaces carry the shorter zoom group of Zoom in, Zoom out and
Zoom toggle.

## Zoom to Fit Project

Zooms so the whole project fits in the visible timeline — from the start to
the end of the last clip. The button is labelled **Fit project** in the
toolbar.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="fit-project" />

Like Fit selection, it appears in the Classic workspace only.

## Zoom Toggle

Switches between two zoom levels, so you can jump between a close-up view and
a wider one without stepping through Zoom in and Zoom out.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="zoom-toggle" />

It is present in every workspace.

## Cut

Removes the selected audio and puts it on the clipboard, ready to paste
elsewhere.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="cut" />

## Copy

Puts the selected audio on the clipboard without removing it.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="copy" />

Like the rest of the clipboard group, it appears in the Classic workspace
only.

## Paste

Inserts whatever is on the clipboard at the edit cursor.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="paste" />

Like the rest of the clipboard group, it appears in the Classic workspace

## Trim

Keeps the selected audio and removes everything outside it, trimming the clip
down to the selection.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="trim" />

Trim and Silence sit together because they are opposites: Trim keeps the
selection and discards the rest, [Silence](#silence) keeps the rest and
empties the selection.

It appears in the Classic, Modern and Music workspaces; the Spectral editing
workspace leaves it out.

## Silence

Replaces the selected audio with silence, leaving the clip the same length.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="silence" />

Because the length does not change, everything after the selection stays
where it is — useful for removing a cough or a click without shifting the
timing of what follows.

It appears in the Classic, Modern and Music workspaces.

## Timecode

<UIExample component="timecode" variant="zero" />

Shows the playhead's current position, and lets you type a position to move
it. Clicking a digit selects it for editing.

The timecode can be displayed in any of these formats:

| Format                    | Shows                                     |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `dd:hh:mm:ss`             | Days, hours, minutes, seconds             |
| `hh:mm:ss`                | Hours, minutes, seconds                   |
| `hh:mm:ss + hundredths`   | Clock time to 1/100 s                     |
| `hh:mm:ss + milliseconds` | Clock time to 1/1000 s                    |
| `hh:mm:ss + samples`      | Clock time plus a sample count            |
| `hh:mm:ss + frames`       | Clock time plus a video frame count       |
| `samples`                 | A raw sample count                        |
| `seconds`                 | Whole seconds                             |
| `seconds + milliseconds`  | Seconds to 1/1000 s                       |
| `film frames`             | Film frames                               |
| `beats:bars`              | Musical position, following project tempo |
| `Hz`                      | Frequency                                 |

Choosing `beats:bars` makes the timecode musical rather than clock-based, so
it follows [Tempo](#tempo) and [Time Signature](#time-signature).

It is present in every workspace.

## Tempo

Sets the project tempo in beats per minute. It is a stepper: type a value, or
use the up and down controls to nudge it.

Tempo is what makes musical positions meaningful — it drives the
`beats:bars` [timecode](#timecode) format and musical [snapping](#snapping).

Tempo appears in the **Music** workspace only — the Classic, Modern and
Spectral editing workspaces do not show it. See
[Workspaces](/manual/manual-index/workspaces) for switching between them.

## Time Signature

Sets the project time signature — how many beats are in a bar, and which note
value counts as a beat. It is a selector: pick a signature from the list.

Together with [Tempo](#tempo), it decides where bar and beat lines fall,
which in turn drives the `beats:bars` [timecode](#timecode) format and the
musical subdivisions in [Snapping](#snapping).

Like Tempo, it appears in the **Music** workspace only.

## Snapping

Controls whether edits snap to a grid, and how fine that grid is. Two
controls sit side by side in the Transport Toolbar: a **Snap** toggle and a
subdivision button showing the grid currently in use.

- **Snap** — whether snapping applies at all.
- **Subdivision** — which grid it snaps to. This button is greyed out while
  snapping is off.

With snapping on, dragging a clip, moving a selection edge or positioning the
cursor lands on the nearest grid line rather than an arbitrary point. That's
what makes edits line up with a beat or a whole second instead of landing a
few samples out.

Clicking the subdivision button opens the grid menu. Musical divisions are at
the top, and the other units are grouped into submenus below.

**Musical** — Bar, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/64, 1/128. A separate
**Enable triplets** option applies to whichever of these is selected.

Musical divisions follow the project's [Tempo](#tempo) and
[Time Signature](#time-signature), so changing either moves the grid.

**Seconds samples** — Seconds, Deciseconds, Centiseconds, Milliseconds,
Samples.

**Video frames** — Video Frames (24 fps), NTSC Frames (29.97 fps), NTSC
Frames (30 fps), PAL Frames (25 fps).

**CD frames** — CDDA Frames (75 fps).

## Record Level

Opens the microphone settings, where you choose the input device and set the
level Audacity records at.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="microphone" />

Setting the level before you record matters: audio recorded too hot clips,
and clipping cannot be undone afterwards. Watch the meter while you play or
speak at the loudest part, and back the level off if it reaches the top.

It is present in every workspace.

## Playback Level

Opens the playback volume settings, which set how loudly Audacity plays back.
The master meter sits alongside it, showing the level going out.

<UIExample component="tool-button" variant="volume" />

Playback volume only affects what you hear — it does not change the audio in
your project, and it is not applied to anything you export.

<UIExample component="master-meter" variant="default" />

The meter turns red when the signal clips:

<UIExample component="master-meter" variant="clipping" />

The volume menu can also switch the meter between a **horizontal** meter in
the toolbar and a **vertical** one shown alongside the tracks.

It is present in every workspace.

> **Tip:** Clipping on the master meter means the mix is too loud, not that a
> particular track is — lower the track faders rather than the playback
> volume, which does not change the exported file.
