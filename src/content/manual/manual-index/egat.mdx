EGAT is shorthand for the four kinds of plug-in that act on the audio in a
project: **effects**, which change audio that is already there; **generators**,
which create new audio; **analyzers**, which measure audio and report back
without altering it; and **tools**, which do a job around a project rather than
to its sound. Audacity 4 ships 54 of them.

## Where to find them

Each kind has its own menu — **Effect**, **Generate**, **Analyze** and
**Tools** — and each menu begins with **Plugin manager**, where installed
plug-ins are enabled and disabled. Effects that can run live during playback
are added instead from a track's effects panel.

## Notes

Some of these plug-ins are built into Audacity and written in C++; the rest are
[Nyquist](/manual/manual-index/egat/nyquist) scripts that ship alongside it.
The distinction rarely matters in use, but it explains why some dialogs look
different from others.

Anything applied from the Effect, Generate or Analyze menus works on the
current selection, so make a selection first. With nothing selected, generators
insert at the cursor.
