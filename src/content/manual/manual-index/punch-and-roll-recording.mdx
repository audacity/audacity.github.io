Punch and roll is the technique for fixing a mistake part-way through a take
without re-recording the whole thing. Audacity plays the audio leading up to
the point you want to fix, then drops into record exactly there — so you hear
your way in and match your pace and tone to what came before.

<UIExample component="transport-button" variant="record" client:load />

It is the usual approach for spoken-word work, where starting cold at the
punch point tends to produce an audible join.

## How to do it

1. Place the playhead where the good audio ends and the fix should begin.
2. Select the track you want to record into.
3. Start
   [Lead-in Recording](/manual/manual-index/header/record#lead-in-recording).

Audacity plays back the couple of seconds before the playhead, then records
from the playhead onward on the selected track.

## Notes

- The lead-in length and the crossfade applied at the punch point are set in
  **Preferences → Playback/Recording** — see
  [Playback/Recording](/manual/manual-index/preferences/playback-recording).
- Recording this way is non-destructive: the existing audio stays on the
  track, and the new take is placed at the punch point.
- Finding a natural gap — the end of a sentence, a breath — makes the join
  much easier to hide than punching mid-word.
