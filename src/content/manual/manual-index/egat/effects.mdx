Effects take audio that is already in the project and change it — its level,
its frequency balance, its pitch and speed, or the space it appears to sit in.
They are grouped into submenus by what they do, so that the ten or so effects
you are choosing between at any moment are near each other.

## Where to find them

The **Effect** menu, with audio selected. The menu opens with **Plugin
manager**, **Add realtime effects** and **Repeat last effect**, then the group
submenus: Volume and compression, Fading, Pitch and tempo, EQ and filters,
Noise removal and repair, Delay and reverb, Distortion and modulation, Special,
Spectral tools and Legacy.

## Destructive and realtime

Applying an effect from the Effect menu rewrites the selected audio. The change
can be undone, but until it is undone the audio in the project has really been
altered.

Three of the effects — Compressor, Limiter and Reverb — can also run live on a
track during playback without altering anything, added from the track's effects
panel rather than the menu. See
[Realtime Effects](/manual/manual-index/egat/effects/realtime-effects).

## Notes

Effects act on the selection only, so a partial selection gives you a partial
change. Most of them open a dialog with an **Apply** button; a few, such as
Fade In and Invert, have nothing to set and take effect as soon as you choose
them.

Some plug-ins are built into Audacity and written in C++; the rest are
[Nyquist](/manual/manual-index/egat/nyquist) scripts that ship alongside it —
and the same is true of the generators, analyzers and tools in the
neighbouring sections. The distinction rarely matters in use, but it explains
why some dialogs look different from others.
