Sliding stretch sets a value at the start of the selection and a different one at the end, then moves smoothly between them. That covers a tape-style slow-down or speed-up, and it also covers a plain constant change: set the initial and final figures to the same number.

<UIShot name="effects/Sliding_stretch" alt="The Sliding Stretch dialog" />

## Where to find it

**Effect → Pitch and tempo → Sliding stretch**, with audio selected.

## Controls

Four cards, each with a slider and a matching numeric field.

- **Initial tempo change** — the tempo change at the start of the selection, as a percentage. Range −90% to 500%, default 0.
- **Final tempo change** — the tempo change at the end of the selection, as a percentage. Range −90% to 500%, default 0.
- **Initial pitch shift** — the pitch shift at the start, in semitones (range −12 to 12) or as a percentage (range −50% to 100%). Default 0.
- **Final pitch shift** — the pitch shift at the end, on the same two scales. Default 0.

## Notes

Tempo and pitch are independent here: you can slow a passage down without dropping its pitch, or bend the pitch while the tempo holds steady.

A positive tempo change makes the selection shorter, so the audio after it moves earlier in the track. Bear that in mind when only part of a track is selected.

For a constant change across the whole selection, set both tempo fields — or both pitch fields — to the same value. This is the effect that replaces Audacity 3's Change Tempo and Change Speed.
