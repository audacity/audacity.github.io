Repairs audio that was recorded too loud and had its peaks flattened off.
Clip Fix finds each clipped region, measures the slope of the waveform on
either side of it, and interpolates a curve across the gap, so the peak is
restored to something like the shape it would have had.

## Where to find it

**Effect → Noise removal and repair → Clip fix**.

## Controls

- **Threshold of Clipping (%)** — 0 to 100, default 95. Samples at or above
  this percentage of the peak level are treated as clipped. Lower it if only
  the worst clipping is being caught; raise it if the effect is altering
  audio that was not actually clipped.
- **Reduce amplitude to allow for restored peaks (dB)** — −30 to 0, default
  −9. The restored peaks are taller than the flat tops they replace, so the
  whole selection is turned down by this amount to leave room for them.

## Notes

Clip Fix reconstructs a plausible peak; it cannot recover audio that was
never recorded. It works best on lightly clipped material where the flattened
sections are short. Badly clipped recordings will still sound distorted
afterwards.

Apply it before any other processing, and expect the result to be quieter
than the original because of the amplitude reduction.
