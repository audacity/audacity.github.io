Lifts or lowers a whole end of the spectrum by a fixed amount rather than
cutting it away. A low-shelf acts on everything below the frequency you set
and a high-shelf on everything above it, which makes this the tool for broad
tonal adjustments — a little more weight, a little less brightness.

## Where to find it

**Effect → EQ and filters → Shelf filter**.

## Controls

- **Filter type** — Low-shelf or High-shelf. Low-shelf is the default.
- **Frequency (Hz)** — where the shelf turns over. 10 to 10000, default 1000.
- **Amount (dB)** — how much to boost or cut the shelved region. −72 to 72,
  default −6.

## Notes

Unlike the high-pass and low-pass filters, a shelf levels off at the set
amount instead of continuing to fall away, so a shelf cut thins the sound
without removing that end of the spectrum entirely.

The frequency must be below half the track's sample rate; above that the
effect reports an error rather than applying.
