Invert turns every sample in the selection into its negative, flipping the
waveform about the centre line. On its own it makes no audible difference —
the sound is identical played back by itself. What changes is how it combines
with other audio, which is what the effect is for.

## Where to find it

**Effect → Special → Invert**, with audio selected. There is no dialog: the effect
applies as soon as you choose it.

## Notes

Two common uses. If a stereo recording sounds thin and hollow because one
channel was wired backwards, inverting that channel alone restores it. And if
you have two copies of the same material, inverting one and mixing them
cancels everything they share, leaving only what differs — the basis of
several subtraction techniques.

Inverting a whole mix, or both channels of a stereo track together, changes
nothing you can hear. Polarity only matters relative to something else.

Applying Invert twice returns the audio exactly to where it started.
