This is the same effect as
[Limiter](/manual/manual-index/egat/effects/volume-and-compression/limiter) in
the Effect menu, added to a track's effect stack instead of applied to its
audio. The controls and what they do are identical — that page has the
reference — but nothing is written back to the track: the limiting happens as
the project plays.

## Where to find it

Click the **Effects** button across the bottom of the track's control panel, or
press **E**, then **Add effect** and choose **Limiter**.

## Working with it

Because the audio is untouched, everything stays adjustable. Change the
threshold while the project is playing and you hear the result immediately;
bypass the effect with its power button to compare against the original; move
it above or below the other effects on the track to change where in the chain
it sits; take it off the stack and the track is exactly as it was.

The limiting is included when you export, so there is no step at which you have
to apply it.

## Notes

A limiter is normally the last thing in a chain, since its job is to catch
whatever the effects before it produced. Keeping it last on the stack is
usually right.

For catching peaks in the finished mix rather than on one track, add it to the
**Master effects** section at the bottom of the effects panel, which processes
every track together.

Used this way the Limiter shows a metering panel above the knobs, so you can
watch how much the peaks are being held back while the project plays. Applied
destructively from the Effect menu there is nothing to meter and the panel is
not shown.

See [Realtime Effects](/manual/manual-index/egat/effects/realtime-effects) for
the stack as a whole.
