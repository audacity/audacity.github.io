Audacity 4 modernises the interface without changing what Audacity is. Your
projects open, your instincts mostly carry over, and where they don't, this
page is the map. The changes that matter most: clips are now first-class
things you select and manipulate directly, effects can run live instead of
rewriting your audio, and the old tool modes have been slimmed down.

## Make it look like home

The first time you run Audacity 4 it asks for an app theme, a clip theme and
a workspace. Choosing the **Classic** clip theme and the **Classic**
workspace keeps things as close to Audacity 3 as possible — the same tools
and buttons, in familiar places. Nothing is locked in: both can be changed
later, the workspace from the selector in the top bar and the rest in
**Preferences**.

## Your projects come with you

Audacity 4 saves projects as `.aup4`, and it opens the `.aup3` projects you
made in Audacity 3 — your old work carries straight over. Saving is
otherwise the same split you know:
[save the project](/manual/getting-started/save-your-project) to keep
editing, export to get an audio file.

## Clips are first-class now

Clips can be selected directly: click one, or **Shift+click** several — they
no longer need to be touching. A selected clip grows handles at its edges,
and whatever you do with them — trimming, stretching — applies to every
selected clip at once.

One behaviour worth knowing before it surprises you: moving a clip onto
another clip is allowed, and the clip underneath is eaten into where they
overlap.

## Effects can run live

The Effect menu still works the way it always has: select audio, apply,
waveform changes. What is new is that effects can also run as **realtime
effects** — stacked on a track, calculated during playback, adjustable or
removable at any point — and as **master effects** on the finished mix.
Nothing is written into your audio until export.
[Apply your first effect](/manual/getting-started/apply-your-first-effect)
covers both ways in.

## Tool modes, slimmed down

The old mode switch is gone: no more Select <Shortcut client:load keys="F1" />,
Envelope <Shortcut client:load keys="F2" />, Draw

<Shortcut client:load keys="F3" /> or Multi-tool
<Shortcut client:load keys="F6" />. In their place:

- **Envelopes** are a visual layer now, toggled with the **Automation**
  button, rather than a mode you switch into.
- **Draw** engages itself: zoom in far enough to see individual samples and
  the cursor becomes a pencil near a sample point.
- **Split** is new — press or hold <Shortcut client:load keys="s" /> and
  click to cut a clip in two.

## Sync-Lock became ripple editing

Sync-Lock is gone; keeping tracks aligned is now part of how deleting and
inserting work. The first delete asks for your default behaviour (changeable
in **Preferences → Editing**), and the sync-preserving forms live on the
keyboard: **Delete and close gap (all tracks)**,

<Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+del" />, and **Insert and preserve sync**,
<Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+shift+v" />. The delete behaviours are covered
in [basic audio editing](/manual/getting-started/basic-audio-editing).

## Knobs

Some controls that were sliders are knobs now. Drag **up or right** to
increase, **down or left** to decrease; double-click resets, and holding
**Shift** while dragging gives finer control.

## Plugins

Supported formats today: **VST3**, **VST**, **LV2**, **LADSPA** and, on
macOS, **Audio Units**. Nyquist and VAMP plugins from Audacity 3 are not
supported yet, and newer formats like CLAP may come later.

## The keyboard, in full

Most shortcuts survived the move, but not all — the
[keyboard shortcuts reference](/manual/shortcuts) is generated from
Audacity 4 itself, so when your fingers disagree with the app, it settles
the argument.
