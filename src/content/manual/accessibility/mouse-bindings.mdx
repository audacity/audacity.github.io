Most of the editing in Audacity is done with the pointer rather than with menu
commands, and the same click means different things depending on where in the
window it lands. This page collects those gestures by area.

Everything below describes normal editing. Turning on the
[split tool](/manual/manual-index/toolbar/tools#split-tool) or the
[clip envelope](/manual/manual-index/toolbar/tools#clip-gain) tool changes what a
click on a clip does until you switch the tool off again.

## In the tracks area

- **Click** in a track to put the edit cursor at that point. Playback starts
  from there.
- **Drag** left or right to select a stretch of time. Dragging up or down as
  well extends the selection to every track you pass over. A plain drag leaves
  the playhead where it was — only a click without a drag moves it.
- **Shift-click** to extend the selection you already have out to the point you
  clicked, keeping its existing start.
- **Ctrl-click** to extend the selection in the same way and, at the same time,
  add the track you clicked to the selected tracks — or remove it, if it was
  already selected.
- **Double-click** a clip to select the whole of that clip. Double-clicking an
  empty part of a track selects all of that track's audio instead.
- **Right-click** for the context menu. Over a clip you get that clip's menu,
  and over the empty canvas below the last track you get the canvas menu.

Dragging a selection edge follows the current
[snapping](/manual/manual-index/toolbar/snapping#snapping) setting, and a vertical
guideline shows where the edge will land.

## On a clip

A clip has a header — the strip across its top carrying its name — and a body
below it showing the waveform. The two behave differently.

- **Click the header** to select the clip.
- **Drag the header** to move the clip: sideways to shift it in time, up or
  down to move it onto a different track.
- **Double-click the header** to open the clip's name for editing.
- **Click the body** and it behaves like the rest of the track: the cursor
  moves, and a drag makes a time selection.
- **Drag a clip edge** inwards or outwards to trim the clip. The pointer
  changes to a trim cursor as you approach the edge.

Select a clip and handles appear at both of its ends, stacked in two rows. The
upper pair trims — the same job as dragging the edge, with a larger target. The
lower pair stretches the clip instead; see below.

### Changing a clip's speed

Stretching a clip changes its speed: the audio plays slower or faster, and the
clip gets longer or shorter with it, rather than more or less of it being
shown.

- **Drag one of the lower handles** — the round ones marked with a clock — on a
  selected clip to stretch it from that end.
- **Alt-drag a clip edge** to stretch rather than trim. The pointer changes
  from the trim cursor to the stretch cursor while Alt is held.

The clip commands these gestures duplicate are in the
[Clip submenu](/manual/manual-index/header/edit#clip) of the Edit menu.

## On the timeline ruler

The [timeline](/manual/manual-index/timeline) ruler is split into two halves
that respond differently.

- **Click the lower half** to move the playhead to that point. Whether playback
  also starts there is set by
  [Click ruler to start playback](/manual/manual-index/timeline/click-ruler-to-start-playback);
  if audio is already playing, a click moves playback there either way.
- **Drag the playhead marker** along the ruler to move the playback position
  continuously.
- **Ctrl-drag in the upper half**, when no loop region exists, to draw one. The
  region appears in the upper half and looping is switched on for you.
- **Drag either edge** of an existing loop region to resize it, or **drag its
  middle** to move it along the timeline. Both follow the current snapping
  setting, with a guideline showing where the edge will land.
- **Click inside** an existing loop region, without dragging, to turn looping
  on or off. This is the same as
  [Toggle loop region](/manual/manual-index/timeline/toggle-loop-region).
- **Right-click** anywhere on the ruler for the timeline options — the ruler
  format, the playback display options, the loop region commands and the
  vertical rulers toggle.

## On a track's control panel

- **Click** the panel to select the track and its audio. Ctrl-click or
  Shift-click adds a track to the selection rather than replacing it.
- **Drag** the panel up or down to reorder the tracks. A drop indicator shows
  where the track will land. The same moves are available from
  [Move track](/manual/manual-index/track-control-panel/audio-track-item#move-track)
  in the track's menu.
- **Drag the bottom edge** of the panel to change the track's height. The
  pointer changes to a vertical resize cursor over the edge.
- **Double-click the track name** to rename it.
- **Right-click** anywhere on the panel to open the track's menu.

The pan knob and volume slider have their own gestures — drag to set, Shift-drag
for fine adjustment, double-click to reset. See
[Audio Track Item](/manual/manual-index/track-control-panel/audio-track-item).

## On the vertical rulers

Clicking a waveform ruler opens that track's ruler options: vertical zoom in,
out and reset, the ruler format, and the half-wave toggle. The rulers are
hidden by default. See
[Vertical Rulers](/manual/manual-index/vertical-rulers).

## Scrolling and zooming

Over the tracks area:

- **Scroll** up and down to move through the tracks.
- **Scroll sideways**, or hold **Shift** and scroll, to move along the
  timeline.
- **Hold Ctrl and scroll** to zoom in and out. The zoom is anchored on the
  pointer, so whatever is under it stays put.
- **Pinch** on a trackpad to zoom in and out.

The zoom commands in **View → Zoom** and the toolbar's zoom buttons do the same
job from the keyboard and the toolbar; unlike Ctrl-scroll, they anchor on the
selection or the playback position rather than the pointer.
