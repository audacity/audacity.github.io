Most recording problems come down to a handful of causes, and most of them
have a quick fix. Find your symptom in the table, try the fix, and if the
problem started before the recording did, work through
[Connect your microphone](/manual/getting-started/connect-your-microphone)
from the top.

## Symptoms and fixes

| Symptom                             | Try this                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| My microphone isn't in the list     | Check it is plugged in and powered, then use **Rescan audio devices** at the bottom of the Audio setup menu, or quit and reopen Audacity. Interfaces must be connected before launch.                                                                                                                               |
| The meter doesn't move              | Wrong input selected, the mic is muted, or the record level is all the way down. Check the input device, any physical mute switch on the microphone, and the record level flyout. If you are on an audio interface, check the gain knob for that channel is turned up and the mic is in the socket you think it is. |
| I recorded, but the track is silent | Audacity was listening to the wrong device. Set the input correctly and record a test before every session.                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| I can't hear playback               | Check the output device, the track's mute and solo buttons, the track volume, and your system volume, in that order.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| The recording is distorted          | You clipped. Lower the input gain and record again; there is no reliable fix after the fact.                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| There's an echo of my own voice     | Turn off input monitoring, or wear headphones instead of using speakers.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| The audio stutters or drops out     | Close other applications, avoid Bluetooth devices, and increase the buffer size under **Buffer and latency** in **Preferences → Audio settings**.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| I can't open my file anywhere else  | You saved a project rather than exporting audio. Reopen it in Audacity and use Export.                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

## Common mistakes

- Recording the whole episode before checking the levels. Always test for
  ten seconds and play it back.
- Normalising before compressing, which brings the noise up with the voice.
- Over-applying [noise reduction](/manual/how-to/noise-reduction) until
  voices sound thin and watery.
- Monitoring on speakers, so the playback bleeds into the microphone.
- Editing out every breath, which makes speech sound mechanical.
