Clicks, pops and crackle come from different causes, and Audacity 4 has a different tool for each. Pick the wrong one and it either does nothing or damages audio that was fine.

## Before you start

Listen through and work out which of three cases you have.

- **Scattered clicks throughout** — vinyl crackle, digital dropouts, a loose cable. Many small faults over a long stretch. Use **Click removal**.
- **One audible defect** — a single scratch or pop you can point at. Use **Repair**.
- **Distortion on the loud parts** — the recording was made too hot and the peaks were flattened off. That is clipping, not clicking. Use **Clip fix**.

## Steps

### Scattered clicks

1. Select the whole affected stretch — a side of a record, or the entire track.
2. Choose **Effect → Noise removal and repair → Click removal**. Start at the defaults: **Threshold (lower is more sensitive)** at 200, **Max spike width (higher is more sensitive)** at 20.
3. Listen back to the busiest passage, not the quietest. Over-aggressive settings show first on drum hits, consonants and guitar picking, which start to sound soft and dull.
4. If clicks remain, undo and lower the threshold a little rather than a lot. See [Click removal](/manual/manual-index/egat/effects/noise-removal-and-repair/click-removal) for both ranges.

### A single defect

1. Zoom right in on the fault with **View → Zoom → Zoom in**, or <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+=" />, until you can see individual samples.
2. Drag a selection across just the damaged samples. [Repair](/manual/manual-index/egat/effects/noise-removal-and-repair/repair) takes at most 128 samples — under three thousandths of a second at a 44,100 Hz project rate — and refuses to run on anything longer.
3. Choose **Effect → Noise removal and repair → Repair**. There is no dialog; it applies immediately, rebuilding the selection from the audio either side.
4. If the fault is still audible, undo and try a slightly different selection rather than applying Repair again on its own result.

Repair needs undamaged audio on both sides, so it cannot fix a fault at the very start or end of a clip.

### Flattened peaks

1. Do this before any other processing, while the recording is untouched. Select the affected audio and choose **Effect → Noise removal and repair → Clip fix**.
2. Leave **Threshold of Clipping (%)** at 95 to begin with. Lower it if obvious clipping is being missed, raise it if the effect is altering audio that was never clipped.
3. **Reduce amplitude to allow for restored peaks (dB)** defaults to −9. The rebuilt peaks are taller than the flat tops they replace, so the selection is turned down to make room and the result will be quieter.

## Notes

[Clip fix](/manual/manual-index/egat/effects/noise-removal-and-repair/clip-fix) reconstructs a plausible peak from the slope either side of the flat section. It cannot recover audio that was never recorded, so heavily clipped material still sounds distorted afterwards.

Click removal and Clip fix both trade thoroughness against damage, and no setting catches only faults. Work up from the defaults and stop at the first that sounds clean.
