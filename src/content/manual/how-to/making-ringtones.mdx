You have a song, a jingle or a recording, and you want a short piece of it as a
standalone file — a ringtone, a notification sound, a sting for a video. The
work is the same in each case: find the section, trim to it, fade the ends so
it does not start and stop with a click, and export.

What Audacity cannot decide for you is the format and the length. Those are set
by whatever will play the file, so check what the device or the app expects
before you export, not after.

## Before you start

Import the audio you want to cut from, with **File → Import**. Work on an
imported copy rather than your only version of the file, and only export what
you have the right to use.

## Steps

1. Find the section. Play through and, when you are near the right place, drag
   a selection across it. Playback follows the selection, so pressing **Play**
   auditions exactly what you have chosen. Press
   <Shortcut client:load keys="l" /> to loop it and keep listening while you
   adjust the edges.
2. Get the edges right. Zoom in with <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+=" /> and
   drag either end of the selection until the section starts and ends where you
   want. **View → Zoom → Zoom to selection**,
   <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+e" />, fills the window with what you have
   selected so you can work on the ends closely.
3. Trim the clip down to the selection with **Edit → Clip → Trim clip**,
   <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+t" />. Everything outside the selection goes,
   leaving just your section on the track.
4. Fade the start in. Select the first second or so and choose
   **Effect → Fading → Fade In**. There is no dialog, and the fade covers
   exactly the selection — the length of what you selected is the length of the
   fade. See [Fade In](/manual/effects/fading/fade-in).
5. Fade the end out. Select the last second or two and choose
   **Effect → Fading → Fade Out**. Play it back: the clip should now begin and
   end smoothly instead of cutting in and out. See
   [Fade Out](/manual/effects/fading/fade-out).
6. Check the level. If the section is noticeably quieter than everything else
   on the device, select it all and use **Effect → Volume and compression →
   Normalize**, which brings the loudest peak up to its default target of
   −1 dB. See
   [Normalize](/manual/effects/volume-and-compression/normalize)
   for what its options do.
7. Export with **File → Export audio**,
   <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+shift+e" />. Set **Type** to **Export full
   project audio**, pick a **Format** — the list shows what your installation
   can write — set the **File name** and **Folder**, and export. The dialog is
   described under [Export
   audio](/manual/menu-bar/file#export-audio).

## Notes

If you would rather not trim the original at all, skip step 3 and leave the
whole track in place. In the Export dialog, set **Type** to **Export selected
audio** to export only what is selected, or **Export audio in loop region** to
export the loop you set up in step 1. Both write the short file and leave the
project untouched.

The Export dialog's **Rendering** section has a **Trim blank space before first
clip** option. Turn it on if your section does not start at the very beginning
of the project, otherwise the exported file begins with the silence in front of
it.

Ringtones are usually played at arm's length through a small speaker, so a
section with a clear, immediate hook works better than one that builds. If your
device caps the length, cut to that limit rather than exporting something
longer and letting the phone truncate it — the cut-off point is unlikely to be
where you would have chosen.

A click at either end after fading almost always means the fade selection was
too short. A few tens of milliseconds is enough to remove the step in the
waveform that causes it.
