Selecting a stretch of audio and cutting it is enough for small repairs. Once
you are restructuring a recording — dropping a section, resequencing takes,
moving an interruption onto its own track — it is easier to work with clips,
the blocks the audio actually lives in. This page covers splitting a recording
into clips, trimming and joining them, moving them around, and the two things
that make those edits land where you meant: zoom and snapping.

## Before you start

You need a recording in a project. If it arrived as one long take it will be a
single clip, which is fine — the first thing you will do is break it up.

## Steps

1. Zoom in until you can see the edit point clearly.
   **View → Zoom → Zoom in**, or <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+=" />, doubles
   the zoom each time; holding **Ctrl** while you scroll zooms around the mouse
   pointer, which is usually quicker. To come back out to the whole recording,
   use **View → Zoom → Zoom to fit project**,
   <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+f" />.
2. Put the cursor where you want the recording to divide and choose
   **Edit → Clip → Split**, <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+i" />. The clip
   becomes two clips with no audio removed and nothing moved — you should see a
   new clip header appear at the split point. With a selection rather than a
   cursor, the split happens at both edges of it, cutting the middle out as its
   own clip.
3. To split by eye instead, turn on the
   [split tool](/manual/manual-index/toolbar#split-tool) in the toolbar and
   click the clip wherever you want it divided. Press the button again to go
   back to normal editing.
4. Trim a clip down to what you want to keep. Drag either end of the clip
   inwards to shorten it, or select the part worth keeping and choose
   **Edit → Clip → Trim clip**, <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+t" />. Dragging
   an edge inwards hides the audio beyond it rather than throwing it away —
   drag the edge back out and it reappears.
5. Move a clip by dragging it from its header, the strip along the top of the
   clip. Drag it sideways to shift it in time, or up and down to move it onto
   another track. Use this to lift an aside or an interruption out of the main
   take so you can mute or process it separately.
6. Turn on [snapping](/manual/manual-index/toolbar#snapping) before you drag
   anything you need to line up. With **Snap** on, clip edges, selection edges
   and the cursor land on the chosen grid — bars and beats for music, seconds
   or video frames for anything cut to picture — and a guideline shows where the
   edge will fall.
7. Join clips back together with **Edit → Clip → Join selected clips**,
   <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+j" />. The clips have to be next to each
   other on the same track with nothing unselected between them.

## Notes

**Edit → Clip → Split into new track** moves the selected audio onto a track of
its own at the same point on the timeline, leaving a gap behind. It is the
tidiest way to separate two speakers who were recorded onto one track.

For a long recording made of obvious takes — an album side, a dictation session
— the shortcut <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+alt+j" /> runs
**Edit → Clip → Split clips at silences**, which does the splitting in one
pass, using the quiet stretches as the boundaries.

Clips you always want to move together can be selected and grouped with
**Edit → Clip → Group clips**, <Shortcut client:load keys="cmd+g" />. After
that they select and drag as one unit until you ungroup them.

Every command above is in the
[Clip submenu](/manual/manual-index/header/edit#clip) of the Edit menu, and
every one of them is undoable. The full set of mouse gestures for the tracks
area is in [Mouse and trackpad](/manual/manual-index/mouse-bindings).
