The built-in [workspaces](/manual/workspaces) — Classic, Modern,
Music and Spectral editing — cover the common shapes of work, but the layout
is yours to change: arrange the tools the way you like, save it as a
workspace of your own, and switch to it like any other.

A new workspace is always made from the layout you are currently looking at.
Set the window up first, then save it.

## Setting up the layout

- Use the cog button at the right-hand end of the Transport Toolbar —
  **Customize toolbar** — to show or hide individual toolbar buttons.
- Move the Transport Toolbar, and show or hide panels, until the window
  suits the work you are doing.

## Saving it

Open **Edit workspaces** from the workspace dropdown in the top right
corner of the window, then choose **Save layout as new workspace**. Audacity
asks for a **Workspace name** and adds it to the list.

Names must be unique and cannot be empty; Audacity says so and asks for
another name if you pick one that is already in use.

Your new workspace then appears in the workspace dropdown and in
**View → Workspaces** alongside Classic, Modern and Music, and can be
removed again from Edit workspaces.

## Notes

- A new workspace starts as a copy of the layout in front of you, including
  the built-in workspace you were using at the time.
- Workspaces do not carry global appearance settings such as the theme,
  accent colour or waveform display options.

**Edit workspaces** opens a window listing every workspace you have,
built-in and your own, with a short explanation that workspaces let you
choose different configurations of the Audacity interface.

## What you can do

- **Switch** to any workspace in the list.
- **Save layout as new workspace** — see
  [Creating New Workspaces](#setting-up-the-layout).
- **Remove** a workspace you created. The three built-in workspaces —
  Classic, Modern and Music — cannot be removed.
- **Reset** a built-in workspace to its factory layout. Audacity warns that
  this cannot be undone before it goes ahead.

## Notes

- A workspace remembers which toolbar buttons are shown and how the window
  is laid out. It does not remember global appearance settings such as the
  theme, accent colour or waveform display options — those stay the same
  whichever workspace you switch to.
- **Tools → Reset configuration** goes further: it clears app preferences
  and custom UI configurations, and deletes custom workspaces and
  shortcuts. It does not touch your projects.
