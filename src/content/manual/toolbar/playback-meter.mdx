Opens the playback volume settings, which set how loudly Audacity plays back.
The master meter sits alongside it, showing the level going out.

Playback volume only affects what you hear: it does not change the audio in
your project, and it is not applied to anything you export.

The meter turns red when the signal clips.

The volume menu can also switch the meter between a **horizontal** meter in
the toolbar and a **vertical** one shown alongside the tracks.

## Meter style

Meter style sets how the bar is drawn:

- **Default**: a single solid bar showing the peak level.
- **RMS**: the average (RMS) level as a solid bar, with the peak level shown
  in a lighter shade beyond it. The average corresponds more closely to
  loudness as heard; the peak is what clips.
- **Gradient**: the bar is coloured green through yellow to red as the level
  rises.

## Meter type

Meter type sets the scale the level is read against:

- **Logarithmic (dB)**: decibels on a logarithmic scale. This matches how
  loudness is heard, and is the default.
- **Linear (dB)**: decibel values spaced evenly along the meter.
- **Linear (amp)**: raw sample amplitude from 0 to 1.

## dB range

For the decibel meter types, dB range sets the quietest level the meter
shows, from **-36 dB** (a shallow range for high-amplitude editing) down to
**-145 dB** (the PCM range of 24 bit samples). The steps in between
correspond to common sample bit depths; **-120 dB** is the approximate limit
of human hearing.

It is present in every workspace.
