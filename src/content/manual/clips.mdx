A clip is a block of audio on a track. Recording creates one, importing a
file creates one, and nearly every edit acts on one. A clip has a
**header** along the top carrying its name, and a body showing the audio
as a waveform: the taller the shape, the louder that moment.

## The clip menu

The **⋯** button at the right of the header opens the clip menu, which is
also available by right-clicking the clip. It holds the clip's own
commands:

- **Rename clip** and **Clip color**, which can follow the track's colour
  or be set to one of its own.
- The clipboard and editing commands: Cut, Copy, Duplicate, Delete, and
  their variants.
- **Split**, and **Split at silences**, which cuts the clip wherever it
  finds silence.
- **Stretch with tempo changes**, and the **pitch and speed dialog** with
  its reset and render commands.

## Handles

Selecting a clip reveals two pairs of handles at its edges. The top pair
**trim**; the lower pair, marked with a clock, change the clip's **speed**.
Both are covered in [Trim and stretch](/manual/clips/trim-and-stretch).

## Clips are non-destructive

Trimming hides audio rather than deleting it: the trimmed part is kept past
the clip's edge, and dragging the edge back out restores it. A clip whose
speed has been changed shows a badge in its top corner with the current
percentage.

## Display

A clip's body follows the track's view: a waveform normally, or a
spectrogram when the track is in
[spectral view](/manual/toolbar/tools#toggle-spectral-view).
